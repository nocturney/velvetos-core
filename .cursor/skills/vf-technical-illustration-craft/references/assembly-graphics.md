# Assembly and service graphics

Use for multi-step assembly/service instructions derived from authoritative product or CAD sources.

- Reconcile the BOM/parts list and quantities before laying out instruction panels.
- Choose a canonical orientation and preserve it across steps unless a rotation is necessary and clearly communicated.
- Keep assembly axes and motion directions consistent with the real operation.
- Prefer one primary action or decision per panel. If two actions must happen together, show why they are coupled.
- Distinguish the new/current part or action from already-installed context using controlled emphasis rather than decorative color.
- Add enlarged fastener/detail insets when the normal view cannot communicate orientation or engagement.
- Show wrong/right orientation only where a plausible user error exists and the source supports the distinction.
- Keep callout/balloon IDs synchronized with the authoritative BOM and detect missing, duplicated or orphaned identifiers.
- Use arrows as action grammar: direction, path and endpoint should describe the operation rather than merely point at an object.
- End a procedure with a verifiable final state or inspection condition when appropriate.
- Keep safety, torque and sequence claims sourced; illustration clarity does not authorize inventing procedure.

Every panel should remain traceable to the same accepted source revision unless the publication explicitly documents a revision transition.
