# Cinematography and shot language

Use for still/video camera direction in Resolve, Fusion, After Effects, Premiere or DCC renders.

- Define the shot's purpose before choosing lens or movement: what must the viewer notice, feel or understand in this frame?
- Specify subject/action, context, shot size, camera height/angle, lens feel, focus depth, lighting direction and motion as separate decisions.
- Choose focal length for spatial behavior and perspective, not because one number is a default. Wide lenses exaggerate depth; long lenses compress it.
- Build readable foreground, midground and background relationships where depth matters; avoid tangent mergers that flatten silhouettes.
- Keep camera movement physically plausible and motivated. A push, orbit, crane, track or handheld treatment should add information or emotion, not decorate the shot.
- For product shots, prioritize shape-defining reflections, edges and readable functional features over arbitrary cinematic darkness.
- Check framing at the beginning, middle and end of every animated camera move and at the final delivery aspect ratio.
- Preserve screen direction, eyelines and spatial continuity across editorial cuts unless a deliberate break serves the story.
- Treat color/grade as support for the shot's focal hierarchy; do not use grade to rescue unclear blocking or lighting.
- If a result feels generic, increase specificity in blocking, lens behavior, light direction and environment before adding adjectives or effects.
