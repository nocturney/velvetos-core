# DfAM lifecycle review

Use before slicing when manufacturing process constraints affect geometry or downstream verification.

- Select/confirm the actual additive process before optimizing geometry; FDM, resin and powder-bed constraints are not interchangeable.
- Treat build orientation as a multi-objective choice across strength/anisotropy, support burden, surface finish, tolerance, cost/time and inspection access.
- Protect machining/finishing datums and add post-process allowance only from process evidence or engineering authority.
- Provide drainage, powder/resin evacuation and trapped-volume review when the process can retain uncured or loose material.
- Check tool/fixture access for planned machining, support removal and post-processing.
- Consider inspection/NDE access when the part has hidden or safety-relevant internal geometry.
- Use sacrificial features, coupons or witness geometry only when they answer a declared validation/manufacturing need.
- Separate process rules of thumb from qualified machine/material limits. Do not embed one universal overhang, bridge or wall threshold.
- Document which risks are digital-only and which require a physical coupon/fit/strength test.

DfAM review can reduce manufacturing risk; it cannot certify strength, material safety or process qualification.
