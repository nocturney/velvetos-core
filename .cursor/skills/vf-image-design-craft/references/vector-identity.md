# Vector identity and logo craft

Use for logos, marks, wordmarks, icon systems and brand lockups where geometry and optical quality matter.

- Decompose marks into intentional primitives and simple Bezier geometry before adding points or effects.
- Use compound paths, cutouts and negative space as real geometry rather than covering unwanted areas with same-color shapes.
- Keep one canonical mark geometry and derive variants from it through symbols/components, swatches and documented overrides.
- Align optically as well as numerically. Visual centers, curve weight and spacing may require small measured deviations from bounding-box centering.
- Reuse curve language across icon and typography when a primary letterform, radius or stroke proportion defines the identity.
- Keep curves smooth with the minimum control points that express the form; remove hidden/duplicate paths, empty groups and accidental masks.
- Preserve live type while choosing/adjusting typography; outline only when delivery or master policy requires it.
- Test the mark at tiny, normal and large sizes, in monochrome/reverse and on light/dark backgrounds.
- Check clear space, minimum size, variant consistency and exact exported SVG/PDF/PNG/AI files rather than trusting the source canvas.
- When an approved canonical brand asset already exists, modify the source/master or use it directly; do not regenerate an approximation.
