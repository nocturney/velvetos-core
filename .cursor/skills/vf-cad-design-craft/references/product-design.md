# Industrial and product design

Use this reference when the task is to invent or refine a physical product, enclosure, dock, tool, accessory, furniture object or other designed artifact rather than merely reproduce known CAD geometry.

- Start with a brief: user, primary function, constraints, manufacturing context, protected interfaces and success criteria.
- Diverge cheaply before committing. Explore many low-fidelity silhouettes and stance families before spending effort on surfaces, materials or hero renders.
- Judge form in orthographic/side views as well as perspective. A direction that only works in a flattering three-quarter view is not locked.
- Resolve cavity and ergonomics before cosmetic polish: where the payload, electronics, fasteners, hands, controls, cables and service access physically live.
- Require a one-sentence mechanical explanation before visualizing a moving mechanism. If the mechanism cannot be explained, it is not ready to render.
- Lock primary/secondary form in neutral clay before CMF. Save early style preferences as constraints, but do not let color/material hide unresolved form.
- Use affordances and signifiers deliberately: handles, openings, controls and orientation should explain their use through form where practical.
- Resist generic AI attractors such as featureless rounded rectangles, decorative seams, mystery buttons or gratuitous vents. Every visible break should have a reason.
- Review assembly order, fastener access, parting/seam logic, material transitions and serviceability before calling the form production-ready.
- Choose materials by functional, tactile, durability, manufacturing and lifecycle needs before aesthetic association alone.
- Preserve a written decision record so later revisions do not silently lose locked dimensions, references or rejected directions.

Do not treat generic ergonomic or DFM numbers as authority. Use current standards, manufacturer data, measured references or owner-approved assumptions for the actual product.
