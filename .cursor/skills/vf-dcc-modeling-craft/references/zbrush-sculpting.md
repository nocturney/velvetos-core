# ZBrush sculpting craft

Use for organic blocking, high-resolution sculpting and form refinement in ZBrush.

- Work big to small: silhouette and primary masses first, secondary planes and transitions next, mid-frequency structure after that, tertiary pores/noise last.
- Keep early topology/resolution low enough that big moves stay easy. Raising resolution too early makes weak forms harder to fix and encourages lumpy surfaces.
- Use DynaMesh while proportions and topology are still changing; once forms settle, move to cleaner topology/subdivision and project detail deliberately.
- Make big proportion edits at the lowest useful subdivision level. Do not fight a dense mesh for changes that belong in primary form.
- Build clay in repeated low-intensity passes instead of trying to sculpt an anatomical mass or plane in one stroke.
- Use smoothing sparingly as a cleanup aid, not as a substitute for understanding the plane or form that should be there.
- Treat mid-frequency structure as the backbone of realism. Pores and alphas cannot repair a weak skull, muscle mass, fold or transition.
- Keep high-frequency detail reversible through layers, morph targets or preserved versions when the workflow allows it.
- Review front, side, three-quarter and stress lighting regularly. A sculpt that reads only from one angle is not finished.
- Save versioned milestones at form gates so destructive remesh/decimation/projection steps are recoverable.
