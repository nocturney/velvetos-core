# Character, creature and stylized collectible craft

Use for characters, portraits, creatures, toys, statues and stylized collectibles after the core sculpt workflow is understood.

- Explain visible forms from underlying cause: skull, bony landmark, muscle/fat mass, compression, tension, gravity or designed shape language before surface detail.
- Fix likeness/proportion problems at the lowest level that can solve them. Re-sculpting pores never fixes a wrong cranial mass, jaw, orbit or silhouette.
- For creatures, design the skeleton, weight distribution, locomotion and load-bearing masses before scales, horns or fantasy surface treatment.
- Introduce asymmetry in primary/secondary forms where it supports life and character; do not rely on random micro-noise to fake naturalism.
- For stylization, choose a shape language and exaggeration strategy deliberately. Preserve clear silhouette, large readable planes and intentional S/C curves rather than simply smoothing realism.
- Use a controlled large/small-detail ratio so one area can carry richness while the rest remains calm. Avoid uniform detail density.
- Keep eyelids, lips, joints and other critical transition zones cleaner than surrounding skin/noise so the forms remain readable.
- Design hair, fur, scales and wrinkles regionally: direction, density and scale should follow growth, compression and form rather than a universal alpha pass.
- For collectibles, judge the pose at final physical scale, including balance, silhouette, fragile protrusions, assembly splits and support/print implications.
- Delegate wall thickness, drain holes, printer orientation and process-specific validation to the existing fabrication/DfAM authority rather than duplicating those rules here.
