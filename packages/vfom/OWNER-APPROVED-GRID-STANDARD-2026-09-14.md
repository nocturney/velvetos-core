## Owner-approved visual language

1. **Product-first** — the physical object is the dominant visual subject.
2. **Real photography as the truth anchor** — start from verified real source media whenever a real product is being shown.
3. **Retouch the photo, not the product** — improve capture, light, crop, background and presentation without redesigning identity, geometry, silhouette, proportions, part count, material, visible surface pattern or verified product color.
4. **Minimal typography** — text is used only when it clearly beats a `NO_TEXT` baseline; Hebrew is short, natural and mobile-readable.
5. **Recipe diversity without visual chaos** — balance `hero`, `UGC/human`, `macro/detail`, `minimal studio`, `bundle/flatlay`, `service/editorial` and process/proof where the evidence supports them.
6. **Feed coherence** — warm refined product photography, restrained dark/cream/neutral surfaces and controlled orange accent behavior when supported by the verified brand/template palette.
7. **Premium but not template-like** — no generic icons, placeholder art, fake retail mockups, stock-looking workshop scenes or visible Canva-template feel.
8. **Photo-first service communication** — even service/brand tiles should be grounded in real Velvet products rather than abstract iconography.
9. **No rigid checkerboard** — diversity should feel curated, not mechanically alternated.
10. **Exact-final review** — approval applies to the quality bar, not to a draft intention. Every public visual must be checked on the rendered artifact.

## Required grid balance

For a typical 6–9 post planning window, the Feed Architect should actively balance the available truthful material across:

- hero / premium product;
- human or UGC-style real use when available;
- macro/detail/craft;
- minimal studio/clean product;
- bundle/flatlay/selection;
- service/editorial communication grounded in real products;
- proof/process when it adds factual value.

This is a balancing principle, not a quota. Never invent a missing category merely to complete a pattern.

## Typography and copy

- Hebrew first.
- Prefer `NO_TEXT` when the image already carries the job.
- When text wins, keep it concise and mobile-readable.
- No generic AI slogan copy.
- No AI-generated Hebrew baked into generative footage when deterministic overlay is available.
- Use only verified brand fonts/templates/colors.

## Hard rejects

The following are incompatible with this approved standard unless the owner explicitly reverses the decision:

- placeholders presented as if they are intended finals;
- generic product icons instead of real product media;
- synthetic replacement of a real product;
- visible subject drift from source product geometry/material/color;
- fake customer scenes, fake shelf presence or invented product variants presented as real;
- text-heavy template cards that overpower the product;
- generic Canva/template aesthetics;
- decorative filler stock imagery;
- the rejected G004 Canva carousel/design `DAHUaelaug0` as layout, style, canonical edit, source or publish asset.
