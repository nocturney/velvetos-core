## Owner-approved visual language

1. **Product-first** — the physical object is the dominant visual subject.
2. **Real photography as the truth anchor** — start from verified real source media whenever a real product is being shown.
3. **Retouch the photo, not the product** — improve capture, light, crop, background and presentation without redesigning identity, geometry, silhouette, proportions, part count, material, visible surface pattern or verified product color.
4. ~~**Minimal typography** — text is used only when it clearly beats a `NO_TEXT` baseline; Hebrew is short, natural and mobile-readable.~~ **SUPERSEDED 2026-09-28** by the owner amendment `Rich editorial layout` below. Kept here for history.
5. **Recipe diversity without visual chaos** — balance `hero`, `UGC/human`, `macro/detail`, `minimal studio`, `bundle/flatlay`, `service/editorial` and process/proof where the evidence supports them.
6. **Feed coherence** — warm refined product photography, restrained dark/cream/neutral surfaces and controlled orange accent behavior when supported by the verified brand/template palette. *(Accent clause SUPERSEDED 2026-09-28: the accent follows the product; see the owner amendment below.)*
7. **Premium but not template-like** — no generic icons, placeholder art, fake retail mockups, stock-looking workshop scenes or visible generic design-template feel (including the retired Canva-template look).
8. **Photo-first service communication** — even service/brand tiles should be grounded in real Velvet products rather than abstract iconography.
9. **No rigid checkerboard** — diversity should feel curated, not mechanically alternated.
10. **Exact-final review** — approval applies to the quality bar, not to a draft intention. Every public visual must be checked on the rendered artifact.

## Required grid balance

For a typical 6–9 post planning window, the Feed Architect should actively balance the available truthful material across:

- hero / premium product;
- human or UGC-style real use when available;
- macro/detail/craft;
- minimal studio/clean product;
- bundle/flatlay/selection;
- service/editorial communication grounded in real products;
- proof/process when it adds factual value.

This is a balancing principle, not a quota. Never invent a missing category merely to complete a pattern.

## Hard rejects

The following are incompatible with this approved standard unless the owner explicitly reverses the decision:

- placeholders presented as if they are intended finals;
- generic product icons instead of real product media;
- synthetic replacement of a real product;
- visible subject drift from source product geometry/material/color;
- fake customer scenes, fake shelf presence or invented product variants presented as real;
- ~~text-heavy template cards that overpower the product;~~ **SUPERSEDED 2026-09-28** — replaced by the narrower reject `text that covers or overpowers the product` (owner amendment below). A rich editorial layout within the amendment limits is not a reject;

## Owner extension · multi-source composition · 2026-09-20

This standard is extended, not replaced, by the owner-approved aesthetic reference `Velvet-Factory-MULTI-SOURCE-COMPOSITION-REFERENCE-v1.jpg` (SHA-256 `78fc8b13593d8bb5806d29e41ea9785625392e00f85e391d864eabc503f14793`). Its role is **STYLE/LAYOUT ONLY**. Products, Hebrew copy, logos, claims, materials, CTA or contact details visible inside the board do not become verified Velvet facts or permissions.

For one exact physical product, multiple verified source photographs may be treated as a single source set. Select one hero and use additional real frames when they reveal different concrete information. A panel shown as a zoom uses `SAME_FRAME_CROP` and is a literal crop of the hero/source frame. A panel from another real frame uses `ALTERNATE_VERIFIED_SOURCE` and must not masquerade as a zoom. Never synthesize a missing angle or detail.

The approved composition vocabulary now includes vertical source rails, circular or rectangular detail crops, rounded cards, asymmetric copy zones, restrained handwritten annotations and editorial footers. These are not mandatory templates. Feed coherence means a shared visual language with deliberate layout variation; repeated identical hero/text/card placement across unrelated products is a repair target.

Copy interpretation follows the same principle: generic camera-angle labels are not a required motif, but side/back/front orientation labels are allowed when the orientation itself adds useful information. When it does not, prefer a concise label for the concrete feature that the view reveals. This is not a blanket prohibition on orientation labels.

## Owner amendment · rich editorial layout · 2026-09-28

Status: **APPROVED** by the owner on 2026-09-28. This amendment supersedes principle #4 `Minimal typography`, the `NO_TEXT`-preferred lines under `Typography and copy`, the orange-accent clause of principle #6 and the hard reject `text-heavy template cards that overpower the product`. The superseded text above stays visible for history. Everything else in this standard stays in force, including every Product Truth rule, the brand identity asset lock and the non-text hard rejects (placeholders, generic icons, synthetic product, subject drift, fake scenes, generic template look, stock filler and the rejected G004 design `DAHUaelaug0`).

**Target language for stills and video.** The reference is the rich editorial language of the current feed posts: owl (2026-09-19), dragon (2026-09-20), deer (2026-09-23) and octopus (2026-09-27). One language covers feed stills, carousels, Story stills, Reel covers and Reel frames.

**Scene.** A warm, real-feeling interior around the real product: wood, plants, window side light, design books. The scene change happens only around the product. The product itself stays the real source pixels (`Retouch the photo, not the product`).

**Layout rules (deterministic overlay, never baked into generative pixels):**

1. **Headline** — heavy-weight Hebrew, RTL, 2–4 lines, 3–7 words in total.
2. **Accent rule** — one short horizontal rule in the product accent colour, attached to the headline block.
3. **Subhead** — exactly one line in a regular weight, at most 12 words.
4. **Chips** — at most 3 chips. Each chip is one outline icon plus a label of 1–3 words. Each chip states a verified fact about this product; no invented features, claims or specs.
5. **Detail insets** — at most 3 inset cards, or one magnifier crop (a magnifier counts as one inset). An inset shown as a zoom is a literal `SAME_FRAME_CROP`; an inset from another real frame is `ALTERNATE_VERIFIED_SOURCE`. Never synthesize a missing detail.
6. **Accent colour** — one accent per composition, and it follows the product (sampled from the product or its scene), not a fixed brand orange. Navy/gold/ivory stays the logo and brand-mark identity (`packages/vfbrand/BRAND-SOURCE-OF-TRUTH.md`).
7. **Mobile readability** — the headline stays legible on the phone-size preview and inside the feed grid crop; subhead and chips stay legible at full-screen phone size; strong contrast, safe margins, and nothing inside Instagram UI zones.
8. **Product dominance** — text, rule, chips and insets never sit on or cross the product silhouette, and never overpower it. The product stays the largest single element and the first read.
9. **Tokens** — accent samples and these layout limits live in `packages/vfbrand/brand-tokens.json`. The owner-supplied logo rasters and the owner-approved traced SVGs are committed under `packages/vfbrand/assets/logo/` (2026-09-28) and are used only as exact files. The verified brand fonts (owner decision 2026-09-28) are Rubik (Hebrew headline 700, subhead 600) and Cinzel (Latin 700/400), committed under `packages/vfbrand/assets/fonts/`; never infer another font family or draw a logo.

**NO_TEXT baseline.** The rich layout is the default. The existing visual-copy process still records a `NO_TEXT` comparison for every text candidate, and `NO_TEXT` may still win for a specific frame when the clean image is stronger (for example a Reel footage beat).

## Tool propagation contract

## Cold-start enforcement

This standard must be loaded from canonical project/runtime authority at the start of every Velvet Factory visual-content branch, including a fresh conversation with no previous chat context. It is not sufficient for the standard to exist only in memory, a previous conversation, or a QA step. Creative Director and Content Sprint must record the canonical binding in `visualStandard`; Brand Guardian and publish preflight must fail closed when that binding is missing or mismatched.


Every tool/agent that performs any of the following must read this standard through `VISUAL-OS.md` / `VISUAL-DNA.json` before acting:

- concept selection;
- image selection or retouching;
- grid/feed planning;
- carousel/post/Story still creation;
- Reel cover creation;
- Brand Guardian / visual QA;
- content production/autopilot;
- final publish preflight.

A conflict resolves in this order:

`constitution / Product Truth / rights / factual evidence` → `VISUAL-OS.md` → this owner-approved reference → local creative preference.

The owner-approved reference is a strong style preference and quality floor. It never authorizes false product representation.


## Brand identity asset lock

For Velvet Factory public visuals, branding is source-locked. Never invent, redraw or approximate a logo/wordmark/emblem. If no exact owner-approved logo asset is available to the job, omit the logo. If one is available, overlay that exact asset deterministically after image generation/editing. Generative base frames must contain no logo, no wordmark, no phone number, no WhatsApp and no contact bar. See `packages/vfom/BRAND-ASSET-LOCK.md`.
