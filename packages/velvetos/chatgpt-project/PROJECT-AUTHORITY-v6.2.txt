7. POSITIVE EDITORIAL DIRECTION
Create a strong hero in a warm, refined, tactile environment with natural depth and intentional light. Use confident hierarchy, attractive typography, deliberate negative space, selective crops and a coherent cream/charcoal/natural-material palette. Accents come from approved sources/templates, not invented brand colors. The product is primary.
Richness is welcome: consistent small pictograms, fine rules, arrows, circular or rectangular insets, short handwritten-style annotations and other editorial signals when they reveal a concrete detail. Do not remove all graphics, turn everything into NO_TEXT or deliver raw photography just to avoid risk.
There is no quota for panels/icons. Usually a small set of distinct details is stronger than a crowded checklist. Graphic detail and a premium finish can coexist. Do not force identical layouts on unrelated products.
A hero+details composition is one option, not automatic carousel construction. If a carousel is chosen, its first slide must be treated; subsequent slides add distinct information.

8. CONCRETE DETAIL MAP AND NO DUPLICATE COPY
Before composition, map each annotation: exact source file + crop/region -> visible detail -> brief wording -> graphic role. Every item must add distinct information, not a second version of the same benefit.
Useful when visible on the actual object: feather relief; the pattern surrounding the eyes; bark-like texture of the base; the open lattice of a photographed lattice object. Do not infer wood material from a wood-like shape. Do not assert print precision/durability from a decorative icon.
Side view/back view/front view are camera metadata, not default editorial copy. Use them only where orientation is genuinely necessary. Prefer the feature the view reveals. Do not invent a new view or zoom into synthetic details.
Roles: headline invites attention; an optional short sentence introduces the object; annotations identify distinct visible details; CTA provides at most one relevant action. Never repeat design precision/print quality/etc. in both paragraph and icon list, even with synonyms.
A factual detail label that helps the viewer notice a source-backed feature is useful microcopy, not the empty image-description narrative rejected by older generic lint guidance. Keep the owner's scoped correction without discarding truth/readability checks.
Hebrew first. Natural, concise, elegant and RTL-correct. Avoid vague superiority and filler. NO_TEXT remains a comparison candidate; it is not the forced winner over purposeful owner-preferred editorial copy.

9. PRODUCT TRUTH: EDIT THE PHOTO, NOT THE PRODUCT
For real-product work use a verified photograph as the edit target, or protect/composite its product pixels. Preserve identity, silhouette, geometry, proportions, parts, openings, surface markings, real color, material appearance and construction. Recognizable is not enough: it must be the same physical product.
The owl's eyes are specifically protected: shape, size, spacing, iris/pupil pattern and color, gaze, eyelids, surrounding texture and existing highlights. Do not enlarge, cartoonize, replace, idealize or add imagined reflections. The same rule applies to analogous identity-critical details of other products.
A textual instruction to preserve the product is not a preservation mechanism. If a generative result changes it, discard that subject and return to the real photo. Use a protected source cutout/mask and deterministic compositing; do not repeatedly regenerate a faulty face.
Do not use screenshot thumbnails, style collages or previous generated ads as a substitute for available source photography. If a source is inadequate, retrieve a better real source or state the exact missing source. No beautifying the product to match the moodboard.
Scene editing may transform background/environment, light, shadows, separation, composition and atmosphere. It must not invent physical use, customer proof, parts, scale or performance. Preserve truthful product finish, including meaningful visible production character.
Macro/detail panels are crops of the verified source set. They may not introduce unseen geometry or substitute another model.
